Analytics in Google give you a clear, quantified view of how well your funnel is working — from the first impression a visitor has on your site through to a completed conversion. Instead of guessing which campaigns, forms, or pages are driving results, you use data to make deliberate improvements. Every metric in the Google analytics suite traces back to a real lead record, so numbers are always accurate and attributable.

## Dashboard Overview

The **Analytics Dashboard** surfaces the four most important top-level numbers in your account at a glance. You can filter all metrics by date range using the picker in the top-right corner of the dashboard.

| Metric | What It Measures |
|---|---|
| **Total Leads** | The number of lead records created in the selected date range across all sources and forms. |
| **Conversion Rate** | The percentage of total leads who completed at least one tracked conversion event in the period. |
| **Revenue Attributed** | The total revenue from conversion events where a monetary value has been recorded (for example, Shopify purchases or Stripe payments synced via integration). |
| **Active Campaigns** | The number of campaigns currently in an Active state, with at least one lead enrolled. |

## Funnel Reports

The **Funnel Report** visualizes your full conversion funnel — Awareness, Engagement, Capture, and Conversion — for any page, form, or campaign combination you select. Each stage displays an absolute count and a drop-off percentage so you can instantly see where visitors are leaving.

To open a funnel report, navigate to **Analytics → Funnel Reports** and select a page or campaign from the dropdown. Use the **Compare** toggle to overlay two date ranges side by side and measure the impact of a recent change.

Drop-off rates at each stage point to specific actions:

- High drop-off between Awareness and Engagement usually signals a timing or placement issue with your capture element.
- High drop-off between Engagement and Capture suggests the form itself — headline, length, or required fields — needs optimization.
- High drop-off between Capture and Conversion indicates a campaign messaging or offer issue that prevents leads from following through.

## Campaign Performance

The **Campaign Performance** report shows delivery and engagement metrics for every campaign step. Select a campaign from **Analytics → Campaigns** to see a breakdown by message:

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Open Rate** | The percentage of delivered emails that were opened at least once. |
| **Click Rate** | The percentage of delivered messages that resulted in at least one tracked link click. |
| **Reply Rate** | The percentage of delivered emails that received a direct reply (email campaigns only). |
| **Conversion Rate** | The percentage of leads enrolled in the campaign who completed the target conversion event. |

Use these metrics together to identify which step in a sequence is underperforming — a strong open rate with a low click rate, for example, means the subject line is working but the message body or call-to-action needs revision.

## Lead Source Report

The **Lead Source Report** breaks down your total leads by the channel or source that created them, so you can understand where your highest-volume and highest-converting leads actually come from.

Sources are grouped into four categories by default:

- **Organic** — Leads captured from visitors who arrived via unpaid search, direct URL entry, or untracked referrals.
- **Paid** — Leads from visitors tagged with UTM parameters associated with paid advertising campaigns (Google Ads, Meta, and others).
- **Referral** — Leads from visitors who arrived via a tracked referral link or partner URL.
- **Direct** — Leads created manually or through the API without an associated traffic source.

You can drill into any source category to see a per-form or per-page breakdown of lead volume and conversion rate.

## A/B Test Results

When you run an A/B test on a form headline, pop-up copy, or campaign subject line, Google tracks variant performance in the **A/B Test Results** report under **Analytics → A/B Tests**.

Each active or completed test displays:

- **Variant A and Variant B** side by side with their respective engagement or open rates.
- A **statistical significance indicator** that shows whether the difference between variants is likely to be real or the result of random variation. Google requires at least 95% confidence before flagging a winner.
- A **Declare Winner** button that becomes active once significance is reached, letting you permanently apply the winning variant with one click.

Avoid ending tests early — wait for the significance indicator before drawing conclusions to prevent acting on misleading data.

## Exporting Data

Every report in the Google analytics suite supports CSV export. Click the **Export** button in the top-right corner of any report table to download the currently filtered data set as a `.csv` file, compatible with Excel, Google Sheets, and any BI tool.

Before exporting, use the **Date Range** filter to scope your data precisely. You can also combine the date filter with audience or source filters to export highly specific slices — for example, all leads from paid sources who converted in Q1.

<Tip>
  Review your analytics dashboard at least once a week. Conversion rates and drop-off patterns can shift quickly after a site change, a new campaign launch, or a seasonal traffic spike. Catching a sudden increase in funnel drop-off early lets you diagnose and fix the issue before it costs you a significant number of leads.
</Tip>
